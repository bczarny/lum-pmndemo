Wildchild leads a 1.6 million-album week as Billy Strings takes indie stores

Physical album sales rose 1.4% over the same week in 2025, powered by CDs and a 12.5% jump at independent record stores. Vinyl slipped 2.4% as direct-to-consumer vinyl cooled.

Alex Warren’s Wildchild was the best-selling physical album of the week, with 44,226 copies — 32,857 of them on vinyl, which also made it the week’s top vinyl album. The album landed at No. 2 on the Billboard 200, and physical sales accounted for 60% of its total consumption for the week. Almost nine in ten of those physical copies were bought online, the opposite of the K-pop titles around it: Stray Kids’ This & That (No. 2, 28,912) sold 92% of its copies in stores.

Billy Strings’ So Much for Goodbyes topped independent record stores, where it was the No. 1 album overall and on vinyl. Across all retail it was No. 3 on the physical chart and No. 2 on vinyl, and physical sales made up 75% of its total units for the week.

New releases dominated. Seven of the top 10 overall physical albums weren’t on last week’s chart, most of them from the Aug. 28 release slate: Billy Strings, The Warning, Prince, Mastodon and Sara Bareilles.

The week in physical

CDs and indie stores carried the week

After a down week, physical album sales bounced back to 1,587,282, up 1.4% from 1,564,882 in the same week of 2025.

CDs led the gain. CD album sales reached 680,017, up 7.2% on the year, after dipping the week before. CD sales at D2C storefronts rose 39%, and at independent stores 15%.

Vinyl dipped. Vinyl albums fell 2.4% to 897,326, mostly because D2C vinyl sales were down 31% from the same week last year.

Channels split. Independent record stores took 34% of all physical sales and grew 12.5%. Mass-market stores took 25% and slipped 1%, their second straight week below 2025.

It was also a week without a blockbuster. For the second week in a row, no album passed 100,000 units on the Billboard 200, and it’s now five weeks since any title sold 100,000 physical copies.

Independent stores

Indie stores had their biggest week in more than a month

Independent record stores sold 546,303 physical albums, up 12.5% from 485,450 a year ago and up 15% on the prior week. Vinyl made up most of it: 375,974 vinyl albums, up 11.6%. CD sales at indies reached about 165,000, up 15%.

Among a like-for-like sample of 360 stores, raw sales rose 38% to 191,762. Same-store sales have run ahead of 2025 in each of the last six weeks.

Year to date

Physical sales are up 6.1% on the year, and CDs are doing the heavy lifting

Through Sept. 3, physical album sales total 51.6 million, up 6.1% on the same period of 2025. Vinyl is still the bigger format at 56% of physical sales, but it’s growing slowly: up 2.1% to nearly 29 million, or 11% if modeled vinyl sales are excluded. CDs are up 12% to 22.4 million.

Mass-market stores are driving the growth. Their album sales are up 28% to 14.9 million, with vinyl up 20% and CDs up 35%. Independent stores remain the largest channel at 16.7 million albums, 32.3% of all physical sales.

Vinyl albums sold by Michael Jackson at indie stores this year, the most of any artist. Thriller was No. 4 on this week’s overall vinyl chart.
